# anibench: second-pass literature landscape

Retrieved 2026-11-17. Every paper below was opened on that date (arXiv abstract or HTML page, proceedings page, or project page). Keys refer to `refs.bib`. Dates are arXiv submission dates unless a venue is given. Where a page did not state something (a venue, a metric formula), this memo says so instead of guessing. This memo extends `locality.md`; it does not repeat the first-pass material except where a new paper changes the picture.

## Summary in five sentences

1. The closest threat appeared ten days ago: **MotionGraphicsBench** (Figma and Stanford, 7 Nov 2026) animates an existing static image from a prompt and grades it with deterministic programs, not a judge. It is SVG and GSAP, not raster and three.js, and it has no separate side-effect score, but a reviewer will ask how anibench differs from it.
2. **Agent side effects became a crowded subfield in 2026**: at least nine benchmarks since February score unrequested or out-of-scope actions with deterministic oracles, and one (SABench) is in the NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track. "Task success is not enough" is no longer a novel claim on its own.
3. **Localized editing with a separate preservation score now exists in several visual-code domains** (Vector-Bench for SVG, MT-Web2Code for web pages, over-editing for code), each built the same way as anibench: known target, protected everything else, binary deterministic check.
4. What nobody does, as far as we found: a **raster input** with **pixel-exact isolation over every frame of a motion**, a **swept-region** allowance, **part integrity and connectivity**, and **pivot** checks, with the grader itself validated against seeded corruptions and independent valid solutions.
5. Image-to-video work on regional animation still measures static-region preservation weakly (user studies, whole-frame consistency, tracker-based proxies), and one 2026 paper (SNF-Bench) shows that whole-frame motion metrics reward drift. That is direct support for anibench's separation of change from preservation.

## 1. Code-based animation and motion graphics (the closest threats)

**MotionGraphicsBench** [ma2026motiongraphicsbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10592), 7 Nov 2026, Ma, Jindal, Wang, Bhakthavatsalam (Stanford and Figma). 100 instances built from real Lottie designs. Input is a static SVG that already contains every element plus a motion prompt; the model writes GSAP JavaScript. Each instance has its own deterministic Python verification program that runs on rendered frames and returns per-check pass or fail. The programs were written with an LLM and checked against human labels: 93.3% raw agreement on 180 held-out animations, versus 83.3% for a VLM judge. 47 configurations from 32 LLMs score 6.0% to 67.7% pass@1 (GPT-6 Astra on top); pass@32 reaches 95%. There is no dedicated side-effect metric. Preservation appears only as per-requirement checks ("required stationarity", which fails in 10.3% of attempts, and "appearance preservation", 18.5%), and the generation rule to animate only existing elements is a constraint, not a scored check. RL is listed as future work. Press coverage on 11 Nov 2026 ([aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/figma-benchmark-scores-32-llms-pass1-spans-60-to-677)) notes the dataset is on Hugging Face.

*How anibench differs.* (a) Raster pixels, not a scene graph: the model must discover the part's extent and its joint, and nothing outside it is protected by construction; in SVG the element boundaries are given. (b) Preservation is a first-class, always-on score over every pixel and every frame, separate from the change checks, rather than an optional requirement on some prompts. (c) Integrity, connectivity and pivot checks have no counterpart. (d) One shared grader with fixed thresholds, validated by seeded corruptions and 242 independent valid animations, versus 100 LLM-written per-prompt programs validated by human agreement. Point (d) cuts both ways: their per-prompt programs check richer temporal semantics (ordering, trajectories) than ours. **Risk:** reviewers will see two "deterministic animation verification" benchmarks two months apart. The paper must cite it prominently and make the side-effect separation the headline, not the judge-free grading.

**MoVer** [ma2025mover](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13372), ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2025, Ma and Agrawala. The predecessor: a first-order-logic language for spatio-temporal properties of SVG animations, and an LLM loop that generates an animation plus a verification program and repairs failures (58.8% correct on the first try, 93.6% within 50 iterations, on 5,600 synthetic prompts). *Differs:* verifies that requested properties hold; does not verify that nothing else changed.

**AnimWeb-Bench** [zhang2026animwebbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09769), 9 Nov 2026. 124 animated webpages to be recreated from video, scored on static appearance and on animation fidelity by tracking UI components and comparing position, size and velocity with the reference. Render feedback helps coding agents. *Differs:* reconstruction against a reference, no notion of protected content.

**VibeVideoBench** [diao2026vibevideobench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35864), 26 Oct 2026 (BUPT, PKU, Kling team, 24 authors). 239 tasks where agents build full audio-visual videos from code (three.js, Canvas, SVG). Scoring is three levels: program checks on delivery specs, about 13k rubric items judged by VLM panels, and 1 to 5 quality scores. GPT-6 Astra in Codex and Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code lead with roughly 75% strict success. *Differs:* generation from scratch, judge-based, no preservation.

**HeyGen Code2Video benchmark** ([HeyGen research page](https://www.heygen.com/research/introducing-code2video-benchmark), 18 Sep 2026; no arXiv paper found). 168 motion-graphics briefs rendered with HyperFrames (HTML or React), scored by a learned pairwise judge with 82% human agreement, reported as Elo. The top four models are within 12 Elo. Not in refs.bib because it is a company page, not a paper.

**SimuScene** [wang2026simuscene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10840), v2 13 Aug 2026. Python code that renders physics animations; a Qwen2.5-VL-72B judge answers verification questions (87.8% human agreement); GRPO with an all-questions-pass reward raises a 32B model to 36.9% Avg@8. *Relevance:* the clearest existing case of RL on code-to-animation, and it uses a judge, not pixel checks.

**Three.js generation.** WorldBench [bakshi2026worldbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10622) (7 Oct 2026, one prompt, five models) is small but useful methodologically: a code-only judge gave full credit to four of five features that had been deleted from the running program, and an agentic judge that controls the clock and checks pixels recovered about a third of the false credit. Frontier3DBench [lin2026frontier3dbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09017) (6 Nov 2026, 145 tasks, 0 to 100 scores; the abstract does not say how scoring works) reports that generated worlds score 83 on local mechanisms but 21 on "motion clearance". A community 3JSBench ([three.js forum, 9 Oct 2026](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/3jsbench-a-benchmark-for-llm-generated-three-js-objects/95137)) reports flickering and invisible surfaces in LLM three.js objects. ManiBench [oli2026manibench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13251) has 12 Manim problems scored by keyword heuristics and is too small to matter. *Differs:* all generate from scratch; none edits an existing image.

**SVG animation by structure.** Vector Prism [yun2025vectorprism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14336) (Dec 2025) groups low-level SVG shapes into semantic parts so a VLM can animate them; the abstract gives no metrics. Together with VAnim and LiveSVG from the first pass, this line treats "which shapes form the part" as the hard problem, which is the raster analog of anibench's grounding step.

**Takeaway for section 1.** Deterministic verification of code-generated animation is now established (MoVer, MotionGraphicsBench). anibench's claim to novelty has to rest on *side-effect measurement on raster inputs*, not on being judge-free.

## 2. Localized and part-level image animation with generative models

These are the natural baselines for a "(d) image-to-video comparison" and the field whose metrics anibench critiques.

- **Follow-Your-Click** [ma2024followyourclick](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08268) animates a clicked region. Its four automatic metrics (first-frame MSE, CLIP temporal consistency, text alignment, FVD) include none that checks whether unselected regions stay still; that is assessed only by a 32-person user study ("Mask-Corr") and qualitatively.
- **Through-The-Mask** [yariv2025throughthemask](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03059) (Meta) introduces SA-V-128 for mask-based multi-object motion but reports only global metrics (FVD, CLIP, ViCLIP, mean optical-flow displacement). No background or leakage metric.
- **Time-to-Move** [singer2025timetomove](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08633) (Technion, NVIDIA) is the best of this group on preservation: a background-versus-object CoTracker distance that detects "unintended background co-motion", plus VBench background consistency. Both are tracker or embedding proxies, not pixel tests.
- **MotionPro** [zhang2025motionpro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20287) (CVPR 2025) builds MC-Bench, 1.1k image-trajectory pairs for fine-grained motion control; the abstract does not describe a static-region metric.
- **Puppet-Master** [li2025puppetmaster](https://vgg-puppetmaster.github.io) (ICCV 2025) generates part-level dynamics from drags with an attention module meant to preserve appearance and background; the project page names no static-region metric.
- **SNF-Bench** [minar2026snfbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28694) (27 Aug 2026) is the most relevant methodologically. It splits fixed-camera videos into static support and dynamic flow regions and reports static fidelity, flow persistence and drift leakage separately. It validates each metric by injecting known corruptions (translation, rotation, scale drift, late freezing) and checking that only the intended metric responds. Its headline: whole-frame Dynamic Degree rises only 1.07x under maximum injected drift, while the static-region metrics rise 1.32x to 1.86x, so whole-frame motion scores reward the corruption. This is the same argument anibench makes about whole-frame SSIM, in a different field, with the same validation logic. Cite it as independent support.
- **What Moves?** [fundel2026whatmoves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04383) learns mask-conditioned local motion representations; no static-region metric in the abstract.

**Layered characters.** See-through [lin2026seethrough](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03749) (Feb 2026) decomposes one anime illustration into inpainted layers with drawing order, trained from commercial Live2D models. Bunraku [chen2026bunraku](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27348) (29 Jul 2026) goes further: layers, per-layer meshes and keypose offsets for a Live2D runtime, with a released Live2D-Bench and an 8,884-model corpus; its main metric is per-vertex direction cosine (0.768). ArtiMo [zou2026artimo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20699) has a VLM and LLM plan joint states for articulated 3D meshes, with a critic loop, on a 225-animation benchmark; Puppet-Master is its strongest 2D baseline.

*How anibench differs.* These systems learn to animate; anibench tests whether a general model can do it by writing a program, and scores the result with exact pixel isolation. Two practical uses: (1) Live2D-style corpora (Bunraku) are a possible source of raster characters with ground-truth layers and pivots, the same thing our synthetic generator builds; check the license (Bunraku is CC BY-NC-SA). (2) The occlusion problem our technical report flags (hidden pixels behind a moving part) is exactly what See-through and Bunraku inpaint, so a "reveal" allowance is a known need, not an anibench quirk.

## 3. Localized editing with a separate preservation score

`locality.md` covered image editing, model editing and code editing up to 2025. New in 2026:

- **Vector-Bench** [gupta2026vectorbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19056) (21 Jul 2026). 40 SVG repair tasks, about 5 requested repairs and 61 protected objects per task. Binary, deterministic: requested repairs must land within attribute-aware tolerances and unrequested structure must stay semantically unchanged. The best of 34 endpoints reaches 15.0% full success while averaging 43.7% repair progress. This is the same "change plus preservation, both must hold" structure as anibench, for static SVG code. *Differs:* no motion, DOM-level not pixel-level, small.
- **MT-Web2Code** [li2026mtweb2code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03474) (4 Aug 2026). 102 web pages with injected defects; agents reconstruct regions or make localized modifications; a dual-axis protocol scores target fidelity and "preservation of unaffected content" with deterministic pixel-grounded alignment. Agents struggle to fix the target without disturbing the rest. *Differs:* static pages, CSS and DOM edits, no swept region or integrity checks. Code and data were "to be released" when checked.
- **When Models Edit Too Much** [zhu2026overediting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04061) (3 Sep 2026). 400 BigCodeBench tasks with injected AST corruptions, so the minimal patch is known; scores excess Levenshtein distance and added cognitive complexity next to pass@1. A preservation instruction cuts excess edits by about a third, and RL gives the best out-of-domain balance of fidelity and correctness. PAFT [yang2026paft](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03113) trains for minimal edits directly. CanItDelete [ebrahimi2026deletion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28887) measures the opposite failure: models avoid deleting code the edit requires (29% of passing patches wrap the target in a guard instead).
- **GIE-Bench** [qian2025giebench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11493) (Apple, 2025) uses object masks to score preservation and finds that GPT-Image-1 follows instructions best but "over-modifies irrelevant regions". DLEBench [hong2026dlebench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23622) targets edits on objects covering 1 to 10% of the image and replaces part of its LMM judge with tool-driven checks because the judge disagreed with humans. GEditBench v2 [jiang2026geditbenchv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28547) goes the other way: it argues standard metrics fail on consistency and trains a pairwise consistency judge.
- **Documents and spreadsheets.** DocOps [jiang2026docops](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19865) (210 tasks) uses deterministic predicates including "preservation predicates" for specified out-of-scope elements (protected styles, untouched worksheets), and lists destructive editing of structure as a major failure mode. SpreadsheetBench 2 [zhu2026spreadsheetbench2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29955) (best model 34.9%) names incorrect target-cell selection as a main failure but does not say it checks unrelated cells.

- **Raster to editable code.** DrawAI [cao2026drawai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00548) (1 Aug 2026) benchmarks agents that rebuild a raster figure, slide or poster as editable graphics code, scored by 39 criteria for fidelity and editability (rule checks plus VLM rubrics). It is the static cousin of anibench's implicit first step: turning a raster part into something a program can move.

*How anibench differs.* All of these are single-state edits. anibench's preservation must hold over a trajectory of frames, with an allowed region that is larger than the part (the swept region) and with a part that must stay whole while it moves. That temporal and structural dimension is what anibench adds. It also means the "family of side-effect-free edits" idea (direction (b) in `directions.md`) is no longer empty space: SVG, web and code already have one benchmark each from 2026.

## 4. Agent side effects and collateral damage

The 2024 roots: AppWorld [trivedi2024appworld](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.850) (ACL 2024) uses state-based unit tests that check for "unexpected changes, i.e., collateral damage"; tau-bench [yao2024taubench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) compares the final database with an annotated goal state. 2026 brought a wave of dedicated benchmarks, almost all with deterministic oracles:

| Benchmark | Date | Domain | Side-effect measure | Headline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agent-Diff [pysklo2026agentdiff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11224) | Feb 2026 | Box, Slack, Linear, Calendar replicas (224 tasks) | Closed-world state diff: any other insertion, deletion or mutation fails the task | Best open model 76% pass |
| OverEager-Bench [qu2026overeager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18583) | May 2026 | Coding agents (500 scenarios, 4 products) | Rule judge (recall 1.00, kappa 0.73) on out-of-scope actions | Claude Code 0% to 17.1% overeager when the consent statement is removed |
| SNARE [qu2026snare](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28122) | May 2026 | Coding agents (10k runs) | Judge-free trap and file-change oracle | 19.5% of benign runs overeager; harness explains 56% of variance, model 21% |
| UnderSpecBench [ji2026underspecbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02294) | Jul 2026 | DevOps (2,208 prompts) | Deterministic oracles: Safe Success, Wrong Target, OverScope | 56% to 68% of runs violate a boundary |
| AmPermBench [ji2026permissiongate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04978) | Apr to Oct 2026 | Claude Code auto mode | 253 state-changing actions vs oracle | 81% end-to-end false negatives; 37% of actions are in-project edits the gate never sees |
| OverAct [zhang2026overact](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01508) | Oct 2026 | Tool calls on private data | Deterministic, judge-free excess retrieval | All 7 models exceed scope |
| SABench [huang2026sabench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34167) | Oct 2026, NeurIPS 2026 E&D Track | Email, calendar, tickets (201 tasks) | Deterministic trace oracle for unauthorized writes | 15.2% of runs; 12.8% of goal-satisfying runs still do damage; 4.7x spread at equal success |
| SideTaskBench [wen2026sidetaskbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14548) | Oct 2026 | Coding (300 pairs) | Unrequested side-task delivery | 20% to 57% conditional delivery |
| ParanoiaEval [luo2026paranoiaeval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08662) | Oct 2026 | Coding (200 pairs) | Unnecessary defensive work, agentic judge | 11% to 59% of runs |

Computer-use safety benchmarks are adjacent but mostly about harmful intent: OS-Harm [kuntz2025osharm](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/49780) (ICML 2025 workshop, LLM judge), OS-BLIND [ding2026osblind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10577) (benign instructions whose context makes them harmful; most agents above 90% attack success), SCOPE [kang2026scope](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22178) (trains a 9B agent with SFT and RL on verifiable tasks with risk variants), and HINTBench [wang2026hintbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13954) (auditing benign trajectories for latent risk).

*How anibench differs.* Every benchmark above measures side effects as discrete events in an environment state (a file deleted, an email sent, a row changed), and the unit is an action. anibench measures side effects as a continuous, spatial quantity in the artifact itself: how many pixels outside a region changed, whether the part split. Its failures are not about authorization or intent; they are about the model's inability to localize. So anibench is a *capability* measure of locality, and these are mostly *policy* measures. That distinction is the defensible framing if the paper borrows the side-effect language. It also means the safety framing (direction (e)) enters a crowded field where anibench's evidence (cartoon hands) will look less consequential than deleted home directories.

## 5. Verifiable rewards for visual code

- RLRF [rodriguez2025rlrf](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20793) renders generated SVG and compares it with the target image as the RL reward.
- UniCoder [zheng2026unicoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31732) (Jun 2026) argues CLIP-style rewards miss fine deviations and parses generated code into symbolic attributes (colors, coordinates) for element-wise rewards across charts, SVG and web pages.
- Visual-ERM [liu2026visualerm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13224) (Mar 2026) goes the other way: a trained generative reward model for visual equivalence, because rule rewards and embedding similarity miss details and can be hacked; +8.4 on chart-to-code.
- SimuScene (section 1) uses an all-pass VLM-question reward; the over-editing paper (section 3) finds RL beats SFT for edit fidelity.

*How anibench differs.* All of these reward similarity to a target. anibench's checks are target-free constraints (many valid animations pass), which is closer to unit-test rewards in code RL. No paper we found trains on a preservation-or-isolation reward for visual output. That gap is real but the evidence that it matters is indirect (over-editing RL result, SCOPE's capability-safety RL). See `directions.md`, option (c).

## 6. Do models know where the thumb is? Part grounding

- PARTONOMY [blume2025partonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20759): 862 part labels; segmentation-capable LMMs do poorly (LISA-13B 5.9% gIoU).
- OP-HRG [mehrab2026ophrg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15374) (Jul 2026): MLLMs ground whole objects well but struggle when the query names a part; a coarse-to-fine object-then-part policy trained with part-aware GRPO lets a 4B model beat 7B grounding models and SAM3 on PascalPart, PartImageNet and InstructPart. A second 2026 paper [jhang2026partpoint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29267) adds a part-point decoder to frozen MLLMs for the same reason.
- PointArena [cheng2025pointarena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09990): about 1,000 pointing tasks plus an arena; Molmo-72B led at release, with proprietary models close behind.
- Vision Language Models Are Biased [vo2026biased](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/vo2026iclr-vision/) (ICLR 2026): on counterfactual images of familiar subjects, counting accuracy averages 17%; models answer from priors rather than from pixels.

*Relevance.* These support the hypothesis that anibench failures are grounding failures, and they suggest a cheap diagnostic: ask the same model to point at the pivot or outline the part, and correlate with anibench outcomes. None of them tests grounding *through an action whose consequences are checked*, which is what anibench adds.

## 7. Grader validity: what reviewers will now expect

- Construct validity [bean2025constructvalidity](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/1967e0fc3aa6cbbace562f5cb8e3954e-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html) (NeurIPS 2025 D&B) reviewed 445 LLM benchmarks and gives eight recommendations; it is the standard citation for "state what claim the benchmark supports", which ICML 2027's DEB criteria now also ask for ([ICML 2027 contribution types](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2027/ContributionTypes)).
- VerifierBench [yin2026verifierbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28679) (Oct 2026) tests LLM-written verifiers against hidden alternative valid solutions and attacks, and finds they reject bad solutions more reliably than they accept valid alternatives, so over-strict verification hides. It recommends testing verifiers against other solutions the task permits. anibench's 242-variant false-rejection study is exactly that, and should cite it.
- Oracle failures [chen2026oraclefailures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16067) (Oct 2026): state-integrity invariants and known-answer controls caught 7 of 7 injected oracle defects and a real reward bug that changed state before scoring; correcting it cut measured success from 94.3% to 65.5%.
- Transcript scanners [mohl2026transcriptscanners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27518) (Jul 2026): automated detection of benchmark flaws (ground-truth access, guessing, format ambiguity) in Inspect Evals benchmarks.
- MotionGraphicsBench's grader validation (human agreement on 180 animations) and SNF-Bench's corruption-injection validation are the two direct comparisons for anibench's own validation.
- Industry practice matches: a Hugging Face recap of lab RL setups reports that GLM-5.3 ships a verifier only after it passes oracle, no-op and unsolved-state checks ([Paniego, 11 Sep 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/sergiopaniego/rl-environments-2026)), the same three tests anibench runs (reference passes, frozen fails, broken fails).

*Implication.* Grader validation with seeded mutants and alternative valid solutions is becoming expected, not distinctive. anibench is ahead of most papers here (two-sided validation, CIs, a selection-bias analysis), but it should present this as meeting the bar, and add a human-agreement number (planned E4) because MotionGraphicsBench reports one.

## Positioning, revised

The first-pass positioning ("visual code benchmarks entangle change and preservation") is still true of most of the field but no longer of its newest entries. The sharper statement, which survives every paper above:

> Benchmarks now verify requested changes deterministically (MotionGraphicsBench, MoVer) and score side effects deterministically in discrete state (Agent-Diff, SABench, Vector-Bench, MT-Web2Code). None measures side effects of a *motion* on a *raster* image, where the model must infer the part, its joint and the region it may sweep, and where a side effect can be a few pixels or a part coming apart. anibench measures that, separately from whether the motion happened, with a grader validated in both directions.

Sentences to avoid in the paper because a 2026 paper now contradicts them: "no benchmark separates change from preservation" (Vector-Bench, MT-Web2Code, SABench, SNF-Bench do); "animation benchmarks rely on judges or similarity" (MotionGraphicsBench, MoVer do not); "side effects of agents are unmeasured" (nine 2026 benchmarks).

## What we did not find

- No benchmark that animates a raster image by writing code, other than anibench.
- No image-to-video paper with a pixel-exact static-region test; the best are tracker distances and region-split metrics validated on synthetic corruptions.
- No paper training a model on a preservation reward for visual output.
- We could not confirm the scoring details of Frontier3DBench, DLEBench's preservation rubric, or Agent-Diff's full model table from the pages opened; claims above are limited to what the pages said.
